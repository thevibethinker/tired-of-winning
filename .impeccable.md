# Design Context

## Users
Progressive friends and family watching election night, mostly on phones, via a publicly accessible link authorized November 3. They want to know which competitive Democrats and left-leaning independents are winning and whether markets and forecasts were accurate.

## Brand Personality
Ironic, exuberant, readable. MAGA campaign-rally visual language repurposed for Democratic wins. More winning means more exhaustion. At 59 Senate seats: “No more winning. We can’t take any more.”

Final copy direction (V, Nov 3 evening, supersedes the lighter pass): title “Tired of Winning?”; openly progressive, sarcastic satire of Trump’s “tired of winning” promise, in his own idiom (Low energy. Sad!, big strong men with tears in their eyes, STOP THE COUNT, find 11,780 votes, Sharpie, covfefe, alternative facts). Target Trump/MAGA, not guests. Keep jokes in headlines, reactions and empty states; instructions, errors and source distinctions remain plain. A compact navy comment strip belongs below the red poll countdown, drawing from the single shared chat with pause/next and a tap-through to the full post.

## Aesthetic Direction
Warm cream paper, campaign red, navy ink, electoral blue. Condensed oversized display typography and ordinary readable body text. Campaign placard meets election desk; no generic SaaS sidebar, purple gradients or decorative stock photography. Color reinforces labeled party/status meanings.

Approved layered direction (V, Nov 3 afternoon, after critique of mockups A and C): "merch table sticker bomb" (mockup C) with A's seat bar and short ladder labels. Bumper-sticker headline; gold level sticker overlapping it; campaign-button Senate counts over a seat bar marked with 51-for-control and the model's expectation; one-row six-step ladder (Warm, Win, Bigly, So much, Tired, No more); luggage-tag race cards with a dashed stub and a tear-off reaction strip; a star only on source calls (gold for our win, ink for a loss) beside the text status; pill dock. Tilt only the bumper, level sticker, seat buttons, current ladder step and call star; race cards stay upright for scanning. Motion is event-driven: slap-in on load, on level or seat changes and on new source calls; no idle loops; none under reduced motion. Evidence: verification/aesthetic-mockups-20261103/.

## Design Principles
0. Frozen final page (V, Nov 7): answers first means final score, then the model-vs-market verdict, then how each race ended. A status strip replaces the countdown; the action bar is Races, Map, Sounds. Locked copy is past tense; nothing implies more is coming.
1. Tell the truth before making the joke: unknown, uncalled, stale and hypothetical states are explicit.
2. Large touch targets and full functionality on a 360-pixel phone.
3. Separate probabilities, vote margins and calls; reveal detailed provenance on demand.
4. Default to contested races with a one-tap “All” view; once votes are in, default to “What’s happening now” status groups (flipped, long shots ahead, nail-biters, favorites behind, lost, too early) so a blowout reads at a glance, with called and uncalled groups visibly different (solid vs. dashed labels) (V, Nov 3 ~10:30 PM); never hide races by our own judgment of what is safe. Default to poll-closing order, with a compact sort menu for model competitiveness, either party's lean, model–market disagreement and state. Label race percentages “Model win %” / “Market win %”; they predict a win, not vote share (V, Nov 3).
5. No original predictions. Scenario controls are illustrative arithmetic.
6. One page, answers first: countdown → winning level and seats → races in reporting order → what-if. The map and the party room are pop-out sheets one tap away (V, Nov 3 afternoon; supersedes “map as central surface”). Forecast and live-call map layers stay visibly separate. No receipts page.
7. Let winning build before exhaustion: six discrete levels on net flips across all offices, with Tired of Winning at 45 and No More Winning at 60 (V, Nov 3 ~10 PM; replaced Senate seats). Senate, House and Governors are equal cards under one flip total, never a Senate-first hierarchy.

## Approved Influences
User-requested ironic Trump/MAGA “tired of winning” theme. No Mobbin reference needed: no unresolved pattern question for this bounded scoreboard.
8. One meter, drawn once per context (live hero, what-if). One display headline per section. Model / Market / Live count is the fixed vocabulary.
9. Sound is opt-in and event-driven (V, Nov 3 ~4 PM): a red “Sound OFF” campaign pin and a dashed coupon card (“Hear him take the L.”) invite guests to turn it on; pop-ups are slapped-on stickers colored by outcome (blue our win, red-ink their win, gold new level, navy other) and always say what happened in plain words before the Trump quote.
